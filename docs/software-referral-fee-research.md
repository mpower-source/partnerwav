# Referral Commissions for Custom Software Projects: Research for the CloudWAV Referral Agreement

Prepared 2026-09-28 for CloudWAV / PartnerWAV. Scope: CloudWAV and its partner MSPs/VARs refer clients who need custom software (apps, integrations, AI/voice-AI, websites) to Thai software houses such as Seven Peaks and CODEMONDAY, and are paid a commission on the resulting projects.

**How to read this report.** Each section is split in two:
- **Sourced facts**: what a named source says, with the URL inline. Most sources are vendor program pages or practitioner blogs, not neutral surveys, so they show what is common in the market, not what is statistically typical.
- **Judgement**: my interpretation or recommendation. It is not sourced and should be checked by counsel or a Thai tax adviser before it goes into a contract.

**Not legal or tax advice.** The Thai tax and Foreign Business Act points below are drawn from public summaries. A Thai lawyer or accountant should confirm them before signing.

## 1. Typical referral fee percentages

### Sourced facts: published programs run by software development firms

| Firm (type) | Rate | Base and duration | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| AssureSoft (nearshore dev) | 5% | Client revenue in the first 12 months, paid monthly on paid invoices | https://www.assuresoft.com/client-referral-program |
| BairesDev (nearshore dev) | 5% | Revenue in the first year; **minimum US$10,000 guaranteed annual payout**; paid monthly when the client pays | https://www.bairesdev.com/referral-partners/ |
| Scopic (custom software) | "up to 15%" | Project value, **on all projects for up to 5 years**; paid quarterly | https://scopicsoftware.com/referral-program/ |
| SmartTek Solutions (custom dev) | "up to 5–10%" of project revenue | Paid within 6 months of the contract being signed | https://smarttek.solutions/referral/ |
| ScienceSoft (custom dev) | Flat fee: $200 when the lead is approved plus $1,000 when the client pays in full | One-off | https://www.scnsoft.com/about/referral-program |
| Toptal (talent/dev) | Flat fee: $2,000 per client that starts paying | One-off | https://www.affiliateprogramdb.com/brands/toptal-referral-partners-program/ |
| HubSpot Solutions Partner (software, for comparison) | 20% | Recurring for 3 years | https://www.hubspot.com/partner-commissions-faq |

### Sourced facts: benchmarks from guides and articles

- Agencies most often pay **10%** of revenue; **5%** is the next most common. Fees are usually limited to the first 12 months of client revenue ("forever" fees are regretted), with a step-down alternative of 10% in year one and 5% in year two. Source: Sakas & Co, https://sakasandcompany.com/agency-sales-referral-fees/
- The B2B agency standard is **5–10% of first-year revenue**, with 10% as a practical ceiling because anything higher pushes total cost of sales above 20%. Flat fees run $250–1,500. Source: https://gigradar.io/blog/client-referral-program
- Consulting referral fees of **5–15%** are common. The percentage falls as deal size rises: roughly **1–5% on large deals with little referrer involvement, up to 35% on small deals with heavy involvement**. Source: https://www.consultingsuccess.com/consulting-referral-fees
- IT freelancer projects: about **10% for closed jobs**, **2–5% for a bare email introduction**, and **15–20% when the referrer manages the client**. The overall range is 5–25%. Source: https://www.freelancermap.com/blog/freelancer-referral-fees/
- Enterprise technology deals pay **5–15% of contract value, often capped**. One suggested sizing rule is a commission equal to 15–25% of the project's gross profit. Source: https://www.referaly.fr/en/blog/referral-commission-rates-by-industry
- Consulting project referrals pay 5–10%, rising to 20% for high-value engagements. The most common structure is 5–15%, and the percentage declines as deals get larger. Source: https://referralhero.com/blog/finders-fees
- Software referral partners commonly receive **5–20% of first-year ACV**. Source: https://www.magentrix.com/blog/partner-compensation-commission-structures

**Size and repeat work.** Several sources say the percentage falls as deal size rises (Consulting Success, ReferralHero). On repeat work, programs differ. AssureSoft and BairesDev cap the fee at the first year of revenue. Scopic pays on all projects for 5 years. Sakas recommends 12 months or a step-down. No source gave a separate "repeat project" rate.

### Judgement
- For project-based custom software, the fair market range for a **pure introduction** is **5–10%**. Most published dev-shop programs sit at 5%, and agency guidance centres on 10%.
- **10–15%** is achievable when the referrer brings a qualified, budgeted opportunity or stays involved.
- **Above 15%** only makes sense on small deals or when the referrer is doing real sales or account work (see section 7).
- Thai software houses bill at lower rates than US or LatAm shops, so their margins may be thinner. Expect them to push towards the 5–10% band.

## 2. Common structures

### Sourced facts

- **Percentage of first-year revenue** is the dominant structure. Examples: AssureSoft 5% for 12 months, BairesDev 5% for the first year, and Sakas and GigRadar both recommend a 12-month cap.
- **Percentage of all projects over a long window.** Scopic pays up to 15% for up to 5 years.
- **Step-down.** 10% in year one, 5% in year two, 0% after that. Sources: Sakas, GigRadar.
- **One-off percentage of the first month's retainer.** 10–20% (GigRadar), or 20% (Sakas).
- **Flat finder's fee.** Toptal pays $2,000. ScienceSoft pays $200 plus $1,000. GigRadar cites $250–1,500. freelancermap suggests a flat fee tiered by project size: $100 for $1–3k, $300 for $3–5k, $500 for more than $5k.
- **Hybrid.** A flat upfront fee plus a smaller recurring percentage. Source: https://refgrow.com/referral-fees
- **Minimums and caps.** BairesDev guarantees a minimum of $10,000 a year per successful referral. Referaly notes that enterprise deals are "often with a cap".
- **Staged payout.** ScienceSoft pays part of the fee when the lead is approved and the rest after the client pays.

### Judgement
A **tiered percentage by project value** is not documented as a standard in the dev-shop programs I found. It is, however, the logical way to apply the "percentage falls as size rises" principle that several sources describe. A reasonable form is a marginal (bracketed) tier, for example 10% on the first US$50k, 7% on US$50–250k and 5% above that. A cap per client or per year is also a common negotiating tool for the software house.

## 3. What the fee is calculated on

### Sourced facts
- **Revenue collected, not contracted.** Sakas recommends basing fees on "revenue *collected*, not revenue *contracted*", paid monthly or quarterly with a lag to absorb refunds. https://sakasandcompany.com/agency-sales-referral-fees/
- **Paid invoices only.** AssureSoft pays "based on paid invoices". BairesDev pays "upon receipt of payment from the client". Scopic pays 30 days after the end of the quarter in which it received the client's payment.
- **Define the base precisely.** Agreements should state whether the base is gross revenue, net revenue or contract value. https://referralhero.com/blog/finders-fees
- **Thai law.** Under the Civil and Commercial Code s.845, a party who agrees to pay a broker "is liable to pay the remuneration only if the contract is concluded in consequence of the indication or of the procurement by the broker". If the agreement has a condition precedent, nothing is owed until the condition is met. Under s.846, if no amount is specified, the customary rate applies. https://library.siam-legal.com/thai-law/civil-and-commercial-code-brokerage-section-845-849/

### Judgement: change orders, retainers and pass-through costs
None of the sources I found set out a market standard for these points. Recommended drafting:
- **Base**: net fees actually collected by the software house for professional services from the referred client. Exclude VAT, withholding tax, government charges, and third-party pass-through costs such as cloud hosting, licences, app-store fees and LLM or telephony usage (important for voice-AI) when these are invoiced at cost or with a small handling margin.
- **Change orders and phase 2 work** on the same project: include them. They are part of the referred engagement.
- **Maintenance, support and retainers**: include them within the commission window, possibly at a reduced rate such as half the project rate, because margins are lower and the software house carries the ongoing delivery.
- **Hosting resold at a real margin**: either exclude it, or commission only the margin.
- **Paid-when-paid**: this is standard and reasonable. Combine it with a statement that the fee is earned when the contract is signed, so the software house cannot avoid it by collecting through an affiliate or by renaming the contract.

## 4. Referral mechanics

### Sourced facts
- **Protection or registration windows.** SaaS deal registration protection is "typically 90 to 180 days", with extensions available where the partner can show active engagement. https://www.zinfi.com/blog/how-long-deal-registration-protection-period-saas/ Secure Code Warrior gives referral partners **90 days** and resellers **180 days** from approval. https://www.securecodewarrior.com/trust/partner-deal-registration-terms-and-conditions
- **Tail period.** A finder's-fee tail is "typically 12–24 months from introduction date". It covers deals that close after the agreement ends. https://referralhero.com/blog/finders-fees
- **Qualified referral.**
  - AssureSoft excludes existing clients and prospects "already in active discussions".
  - BairesDev rejects referrals of existing clients or companies already in active discussions.
  - ScienceSoft requires a brand-new customer.
  - Scopic pays once the client "has successfully signed a contract".
  - SmartTek requires an NDA, an introduction, and a scheduled introduction call.
- **Pre-existing relationships.** Existing clients are excluded (AssureSoft, BairesDev). AssureSoft reviews former inactive clients case by case.
- **Non-circumvention.** This clause prevents the recipient from bypassing the finder to avoid the fee. The agreement must be in writing *before* the introduction. https://referralhero.com/blog/finders-fees For enforceability, name the covered parties and affiliates and set reasonable time and geographic limits. https://aaronhall.com/non-circumvention-clauses-in-referral-agreements/
- **Thai conflict rule.** Under CCC s.847, a broker loses the right to remuneration if, contrary to the engagement, it "acted also for the third party or has been promised by such third party a remuneration which is not consistent with the broker acting in good faith". This matters if CloudWAV or an MSP is also paid by the client, for example as the client's IT adviser.

### Judgement: recommended mechanics
- **Registration**: registered through the PartnerWAV portal, timestamped, and protected for **6 months** from acceptance. It extends automatically while there is documented active engagement, such as a proposal, a meeting or an SOW in negotiation.
- **Acceptance**: the software house has **5 business days** to accept or reject the registration. It may reject only with written evidence of a prior relationship: an existing client, or documented active discussions within the previous **6 months**. If it does not respond in time, the registration is deemed accepted.
- **Tail and non-circumvention**: if the registered client signs any engagement with the software house or its affiliates within **12 months** of the introduction, or within the protection window if that is longer, the fee applies.
- **Non-solicitation**: each side agrees not to solicit the other's clients or staff for 12 months.
- **MSP conflicts**: require the MSP to disclose the fee to the client when the MSP is acting as the client's trusted adviser. This addresses the s.847 risk and is also good practice.

## 5. Payment terms

### Sourced facts
- **Timing.**
  - AssureSoft and BairesDev pay monthly on paid invoices.
  - Scopic pays quarterly, 30 days after the end of the quarter.
  - HubSpot pays quarterly, 45 days after quarter end.
  - Finder's-fee guidance: commonly 30 days after the trigger event (ReferralHero).
- **Hold period and clawback.** Refgrow recommends a 30–60-day hold, longer than any refund window. https://refgrow.com/referral-fees Sakas recommends a payout lag to absorb refunds. HubSpot stops paying commission if the customer does not pay.
- **Documentation deadline.** HubSpot forfeits the revenue share if the required documentation is not submitted within six months of the deal closing.
- **Currency.** HubSpot pays in the currency of the HubSpot entity the partner enrolled with (USD, EUR, SGD and others). https://www.hubspot.com/partner-commissions-faq
- **Late payment.** "Late referral fee payments destroy program credibility." (Refgrow)

### Judgement: recommended terms
- **Timing**: pay within **30 days after the end of the month** in which the software house receives the client's payment.
- **Reporting**: a monthly statement listing invoices, amounts collected, the commission base and any exclusions.
- **Audit right**: once a year, on 30 days' notice. The software house bears the audit cost if underpayment exceeds 5%.
- **Clawback**: limited to amounts actually refunded or credited to the client within 90 days.
- **Currency**: the currency the client paid in, or THB at the Bank of Thailand reference rate on the payment date. Tax gross-up clause as in section 8.

## 6. Two-tier arrangements: operator plus sub-referrer (MSP)

### Sourced facts
- **Master agents / TSDs.** The model is that "the provider pays commissions to the master agent, who takes a percentage and passes the remainder to the sub-agent". https://www.unifyr.com/atlas/master-agent/
- **Typical splits.** Splits in the TSD market run from **85/15 to 95/5 in the sub-agent's favour**, and many brokers work at **90/10**. https://channelplaybook.com/telecom/a-special-kind-of-tsd/ and https://channelpulse.io/articles/master-agent-vs-tsd-vs-distributor/ These figures come from recurring telecom and cloud commissions, where the TSD provides tools and support but does not deliver the service.
- **Affiliate marketing.** Two-tier affiliate programs usually pay the second tier a small override; see for example https://track360.io/glossary/multi-tier-commission. I found no published standard for services referrals.

### Judgement
CloudWAV does more than a TSD: it vets and packages the software houses, runs the portal, handles registration and disputes, and possibly does pre-sales scoping. A **70/30 to 80/20 split in the MSP's favour** of the total referral fee is defensible when the MSP originates the client. If CloudWAV does material pre-sales work, such as scoping workshops or solution design, use 60/40.

Two drafting points:
- The software house should pay CloudWAV only. CloudWAV then pays the MSP under its own partner terms. This gives one contract, one tax flow and one non-circumvention covenant.
- The agreement with the software house should extend non-circumvention to clients introduced by CloudWAV's **sub-referrers**.

## 7. How fees rise with deeper involvement

### Sourced facts
- **Fees scale with the referrer's role.** Fees rise from a quick email introduction (lower percentage) to active participation in closing (higher percentage), up to 35% on small, high-involvement deals. (Consulting Success)
- **Freelance projects.** 2–5% for an introduction, about 10% for a closed job, 15–20% when the referrer manages the client. (freelancermap)
- **Reseller discounts.** Software channels "often run 15–40 percent off list at the reseller level". Deal-registration uplifts add 5–15%. Referral partners, who pass leads but do not transact, get 5–20% of first-year ACV. (Magentrix)
- **White-label resale of development work.** The "typical markup is 50% to 70% over partner cost" when the agency owns the client and delivery. Reseller patterns sit at the low end because the partner shop does more of the project management. https://cadence.withremote.ai/blog/white-label-development-services

### Judgement: a three-level ladder

| Level | CloudWAV/MSP role | Economics |
|---|---|---|
| **Referral only** | Warm introduction and registration. The software house sells and delivers. | 5–10% of collected fees |
| **Co-sell / managed referral** | Joint scoping, solution design (especially AI/voice-AI integration with MSP infrastructure), commercial negotiation, ongoing account and relationship management, first-line escalation | 12–20% |
| **Reseller / white-label** | CloudWAV or the MSP contracts with the client, invoices, carries delivery and credit risk, and subcontracts to the software house | 20–40% markup on the software house's price (lower than the 50–70% US white-label figure, because Thai client price sensitivity and the software house doing the project management both compress margin) |

## 8. Thailand specifics

### Sourced facts

**Withholding tax on commissions paid to Thai companies: 3%.**
- Revenue Departmental Order Por.4/2528: commissions and brokerage fees (s.40(2)) paid to companies or juristic partnerships are subject to "withholding income tax at a rate of 3.0 percent". Service fees (s.40(8)) are also 3%. https://www.rd.go.th/fileadmin/user_upload/kormor/eng/RDR_4.pdf
- Confirmed by Lorenz & Partners ("3% of the payment" for fees, brokerages and commission fees): https://www.lorenz-partners.com/newsletter-no-3-en/
- The 3% withholding is filed on PND.53 for companies and PND.3 for individuals, and generally applies once payments under a contract reach **THB 1,000**. https://www.thaicore.co.th/en/thailand-withholding-tax/
- The temporary 1% e-WHT incentive expired on **31 Dec 2025** (Thaicore).

**Withholding tax on payments to foreign companies not carrying on business in Thailand: 15% under domestic law.**
- Lorenz & Partners gives 15% for services.
- The Revenue Department lists 15% for "interests, royalties, capital gains, rents and professional fees". https://www.rd.go.th/english/20470.html
- The payment is filed on PND.54.
- Reliance Consulting gives 15% gross as the standard rate and notes that a certificate of residence is needed to claim treaty relief. https://www.relianceconsulting.co.th/handling-withholding-tax-on-foreign-vendor-payments-in-thailand/

**Treaty relief for a US recipient.**
- Many Thai double tax agreements treat service income as business profits. On that basis, withholding "may be exempted" where the foreign provider has no permanent establishment in Thailand. https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/withholding-tax-in-thailand-guide-for-foreign-service-providers/
- Under Article 7 of the US–Thailand treaty, business profits of a US company are not taxable in Thailand unless it has a permanent establishment there. Thai domestic law still imposes withholding on such payments. https://www.hlbthai.com/double-tax-agreement-between-the-united-states-of-america-and-thailand-including-examples/
- Treaty text: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/thailand.pdf

**VAT.**
- VAT is **7%** (6.3% plus local tax). On 27 July 2026 the Cabinet extended the reduced rate from 1 October 2026 to **30 September 2027**. The statutory rate would otherwise be 10%. https://www.nationthailand.com/news/policy/40069105 and https://www.nishimura.com/en/knowledge/publications/20251022-116401
- When a Thai VAT-registered business buys services from an overseas provider, it self-assesses **7% VAT on PP.36** by the 7th of the following month (15th if filed online), and can claim it back as input VAT. https://unionspace.co.th/doing-business-living-bangkok/vat-and-withholding-tax-when-purchasing-a-services-from-overseas-service-provider/
- Withholding tax and VAT on overseas services are separate analyses (Thaicore).

**Foreign Business Act.**
- "Brokerage or agency businesses" are restricted for foreign-owned businesses under List 3, item 11. The exceptions cover securities, production inputs, large-capital international trade (THB 100M or more), and categories set by ministerial regulation. https://library.siam-legal.com/thai-law/foreign-business-act-types-of-businesses-list-3/
- The **28 August 2026** ministerial regulations exempted only some derivatives agents from the brokerage and agency category. There is no general exemption for commercial referral. https://www.mondaq.com/contracts-and-commercial-law/1837188/thailand-opens-certain-services-and-other-activities-to-foreign-business
- Other service businesses fall under List 3(21) generally.

**Civil and Commercial Code ss.845–849.** These govern brokerage: payment is due only when the contract concludes, customary rates apply if no amount is fixed, and the broker forfeits the fee if it acts for the other side. See section 3.

**Thai-market commission rates: none found.** I found no published referral program or benchmark for Thai software houses, including on the Seven Peaks and CODEMONDAY websites or in Thai-language searches. Rates will have to be negotiated.

### Judgement: tax and legal flow for CloudWAV
1. **The Thai software house pays CloudWAV's Thai company, if Thai-majority owned.**
   - The software house withholds 3% (PND.53) and issues a 50 Tawi certificate. CloudWAV credits it against corporate income tax.
   - If CloudWAV Thailand is VAT-registered, it adds 7% VAT to its commission invoice. The software house recovers this as input VAT.
   - This is the cleanest route for Thai-client deals.
2. **The Thai software house pays CloudWAV's US company.**
   - By default the software house withholds 15% (PND.54) and also self-assesses 7% VAT on PP.36.
   - CloudWAV can claim treaty exemption under Article 7 by providing a US certificate of residence (normally IRS Form 6166; the form number is not verified in this research). Some payers still withhold by default, so negotiate this in advance.
   - Put a **tax clause** in the agreement: fees are stated exclusive of VAT; the payer delivers withholding certificates within 30 days; and the parties cooperate on treaty relief. A full gross-up is unlikely to be accepted, but it is worth asking for.
3. **Foreign Business Act risk.** If CloudWAV operates a **foreign-majority Thai entity** that earns referral commissions in Thailand, that activity may fall under List 3 "brokerage or agency" and need a Foreign Business Licence or BOI promotion. A US entity paid cross-border for introductions arranged from abroad is a weaker nexus case. Either way, **get Thai counsel sign-off**.
4. **MSP sub-referrers in Thailand.** Thai companies paid by CloudWAV Thailand are subject to 3% withholding. Thai individuals follow PND.3 rules. Foreign MSPs follow PND.54 rules, with treaty relief possible.

## 9. Three recommended fee models for CloudWAV

All three models share these terms, based on sections 3 to 5:
- The fee is calculated on net professional-services fees **actually collected**, excluding VAT, withholding tax and pass-through costs.
- Payment is due within 30 days of month end.
- 6-month registration protection, 12-month tail and non-circumvention.
- Clawback limited to refunds.
- Annual audit right.
- The software house pays CloudWAV only. CloudWAV pays MSPs.

### Model A: "Standard Referral", with a flat percentage for 12 months
- **10%** of collected fees from the referred client for **12 months from the first invoice**. This covers all projects, change orders and retainers in that period.
- **Step-down**: 5% in months 13 to 24 on continuing work.
- **MSP split**: 70% to the originating MSP and 30% to CloudWAV. That is 7% and 3% of the fees.
- **Pros**: sits on the most-cited agency benchmarks (Sakas and GigRadar at 10% for the first year with a step-down). Simple to administer in the portal. Easy for software houses to accept.
- **Cons**: large enterprise deals overpay relative to effort. CloudWAV's 3% cut is thin on small projects. The software house may argue for 5%, which is what AssureSoft and BairesDev pay.

### Model B: "Tiered by Project Value", with a minimum and a cap
- Marginal brackets per client for the first 24 months:
  - **12%** on the first US$50k (about THB 1.6M)
  - **8%** on US$50k–250k
  - **5%** above US$250k
- **Minimum fee** of US$500 per closed project, to cover small website builds.
- **Cap** of US$50k per client per year. This is negotiable and can be offered as a concession.
- Maintenance and retainers commissioned at **half rate**.
- **MSP split**: 75/25.
- **Pros**: reflects the "percentage falls as deal size rises" principle (Consulting Success, ReferralHero). Attractive to MSPs on the small and mid-size deals they mostly bring. Protects the software house's margin on large AI programmes.
- **Cons**: harder to explain and to calculate. Needs clear bracket accounting in the reporting.

### Model C: "Involvement Ladder", combining referral, co-sell and resell
- **Referral only**: 7% of collected fees for 12 months.
- **Co-sell**: **15%** for 24 months. Applies when CloudWAV or the MSP leads scoping or discovery, produces or co-writes the solution design (for example, voice-AI integration with the client's phone system or CRM), joins the commercial close, and provides named account management. The level is agreed per deal at registration.
- **Resell or white-label**: CloudWAV or the MSP contracts with the client. The software house gives a **20–25% partner discount** off its list rate, and CloudWAV sets the client price, targeting a total margin of 25–40%. CloudWAV carries credit and delivery-management risk.
- **MSP split in co-sell**: 60/40 when CloudWAV does the solution design, 80/20 when the MSP does.
- **Pros**: pays for real work, as the freelancermap, Consulting Success, Magentrix and Cadence sources describe. Gives CloudWAV a path to higher-margin AI and voice-AI integration revenue. Fits PartnerWAV's multi-tier channel positioning.
- **Cons**: needs clear written definitions of each level to avoid disputes. Reselling adds contract, credit, tax and Foreign Business Act exposure for CloudWAV. The software house may resist 15% without evidence of CloudWAV's pre-sales capability.

**My recommendation (judgement).** Launch with **Model C**, using **Model A's 10% as the default referral rate** rather than 7%, so partners see a headline number that matches the market. Offer Model B's tiering as a negotiating concession for software houses that expect large enterprise deals. Confirm with Thai counsel which CloudWAV entity receives fees, for the 3% or 15% withholding and Foreign Business Act reasons in section 8, before signing.

## Gaps and caveats
- I found no Thai-market referral rates.
- I found no primary source for how the master-agent split works for *services* rather than telecom.
- No source set a market standard for change orders or pass-through costs; those recommendations are judgement.
- The Form 6166 reference and Thailand's VAT registration threshold were not verified in this research.
- Most benchmark sources are vendor or practitioner blogs, not surveys.
